## Access, version and reuse summary

- **Official-primary preference:** federal statutes/regulations and Nebraska statutes were inspected on official government hosts. Georgia code volumes are official legislature-hosted PDFs; some sections were extractable and some (e.g., §34-1-2) were not cleanly extractable in this run.
- **Secondary/limited sources are marked.** The Georgia USCR 21 text came from a secondary verbatim reproduction; the juvenile statute was identified through a county official page. Neither is treated as sufficient for a final legal claim without official-text verification and qualified review.
- **EEOC correction (2026-11-23, independent Codex check).** The original builder lookup found no official rescission notice and marked the 2012 guidance disputed. Codex's independent check found the EEOC's 2026-10-29 announcement reporting the vote and immediate rescission. The announcement is now the primary outcome source (L3a); the 2012 guidance is historical/rescinded (L3). The original failed lookup is preserved in this history. The Q&A and 2014 joint guidance are separately qualified and are not assumed rescinded. The Federal Register cancellation notice (L3b) corroborates the notation-vote process only.
- **Version monitoring:** Georgia code volumes lag the current session; Nebraska statute pages include amendment/source notes and effective dates; federal text needs a current U.S. Code check; appellate opinions are subject to rehearing/correction. Each future legal claim must record the source version/date actually used.
- **Reuse:** federal statutes/regulations and court opinions are generally public-domain government text, but website terms still apply. Georgia annotated code and vendor/legal-aid pages may carry copyright or use restrictions; do not redistribute content. Do not scrape or bulk-collect any source.
- **No legal conclusions:** this inventory does not decide whether a record qualifies for restriction/set-aside, whether an employer violated the FCRA/Title VII, or which notice response is legally sufficient. Those require qualified legal review and user-confirmed facts.
